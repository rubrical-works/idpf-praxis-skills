## Baseline

Primary agent's initial read: **lean for-claim.** *MFW* requires the two protections (independent special committee + informed, uncoerced majority-of-the-minority vote) to be conditioned on the deal *ab initio*. Chancery decisions reading *ab initio* narrowly have required the conditioning to be in place at first economic outreach by the controller, not merely at any point before substantive negotiations. But where the special committee preexisted as a strategic-alternatives committee with broad mandate, Chancery has generally treated the pre-bid empanelment as satisfying the *ab initio* condition. One grounding citation: *Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635, 644-45 (Del. 2014) (`https://example.com/del-supreme/kahn-mfw-2014`), establishing the dual-protection framework.

## For-Brief

### Core Position

A special committee empaneled before any controller bid — particularly one with a broad strategic-alternatives mandate that includes considering a sale to the controller as one of several options — satisfies the *ab initio* requirement of *MFW*. The committee's *prior* existence is stronger evidence of independence than a committee created in response to a bid, because the latter is structurally responsive to the controller's initiative while the former is structurally not.

## Against-Brief

### Core Position

A literal reading of *Synutra* requires the conditioning to be embedded in the controller's *first overture*, not merely satisfied by the committee's prior existence. *Flood v. Synutra* and post-*Synutra* Chancery decisions have rejected attempts to read the timing requirement as forgiving where the controller's first overture did not explicitly impose both *MFW* conditions, even when an independent committee was already in place.
